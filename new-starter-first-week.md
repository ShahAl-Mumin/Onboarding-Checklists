# New Starter: First Week Checklist

A practical checklist for making a new starter's first week calm, organised and welcoming. Written for HR teams, hiring managers, IT and L&D in UK organisations. Copy it, adapt it and make it yours.

> [!NOTE]
> **Last reviewed:** October 2026. This is a general template, not legal advice. Adapt it to your organisation's policies and contracts.

## How to use this

- Owners are suggested in brackets. Change them to match your teams.
- Start section 1 the moment the offer is accepted, not the week before. Most first-week problems (late laptops, missing access, the wrong induction date) begin with a request raised too late.
- Share status, not sensitive detail. A hiring manager needs to know a check is complete, not what it involved.

---

## 1. As soon as the offer is accepted

- [ ] Start date confirmed in writing with the new starter and the hiring manager (HR)
- [ ] Contract issued and returned signed (HR)
- [ ] Pre-employment checks started: right to work, references, and any role-specific checks such as DBS or occupational health (HR)
- [ ] Equipment and access requested from IT, including role-specific systems (Hiring manager / IT)
- [ ] Induction and mandatory training dates requested (L&D)
- [ ] Payroll starter information requested (HR / Payroll)
- [ ] A named point of contact given to the new starter (HR)

## 2. In the weeks before day one

- [ ] A short update sent each time a milestone completes, so the starter is never left guessing (HR)
- [ ] Welcome message from the hiring manager (Hiring manager)
- [ ] Buddy or "first-week friend" assigned (Hiring manager)
- [ ] Reasonable adjustments agreed and arranged if requested (HR / Hiring manager)
- [ ] Written statement of employment particulars issued (HR; check the current legal requirements)
- [ ] Starter details submitted to payroll before the cut-off (HR / Payroll)
- [ ] Pension auto-enrolment assessment completed (HR / Payroll)

## 3. The week before

- [ ] Laptop and equipment ready and tested (IT)
- [ ] Accounts created: email, core systems, building pass (IT / Facilities)
- [ ] Workspace ready, and a display screen equipment (DSE) assessment arranged (Facilities / Hiring manager)
- [ ] Induction confirmed with the correct date, time and location (L&D)
- [ ] First-week plan written and shared with the starter (Hiring manager)
- [ ] Team told who is joining and when (Hiring manager)
- [ ] "Day one" email sent: start time, where to go, who to ask for, travel and parking, dress code (HR / Hiring manager)

## 4. Day one

- [ ] Warm welcome at reception, with someone waiting for them (Hiring manager)
- [ ] Equipment handed over and logins tested together before the starter is left alone (IT)
- [ ] Fire safety, health and safety, and a building tour (Facilities / L&D)
- [ ] Introductions to the team and the buddy; lunch arranged (Hiring manager)
- [ ] Expectations conversation: the role, priorities and how the first weeks will work (Hiring manager)
- [ ] Key policies and the handbook shared, with time to read them (HR)
- [ ] End-of-day check-in: "How was your first day? Is anything missing?" (Hiring manager)

## 5. Days two to four

- [ ] Mandatory training started: data protection, information security, equality and diversity, health and safety (L&D)
- [ ] Role-specific training or shadowing booked (Hiring manager)
- [ ] Introductions to key colleagues and stakeholders (Hiring manager)
- [ ] A first small piece of real work given, with clear success criteria (Hiring manager)
- [ ] Benefits, pension and wellbeing information shared (HR)
- [ ] A five-minute check-in each day (Hiring manager)

## 6. End of the first week

- [ ] One-to-one review: what went well, what was confusing, what was missing (Hiring manager)
- [ ] Probation objectives or a 30/60/90-day plan agreed (Hiring manager)
- [ ] Feedback on the onboarding experience recorded (HR)
- [ ] Outstanding access, equipment or training gaps escalated, each with an owner (HR / IT / L&D)
- [ ] Next one-to-one booked (Hiring manager)

## Where first weeks usually go wrong

| What goes wrong | Usual cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Laptop not ready | Ordered late | Request it when the offer is accepted, not when the start date is close |
| No system access on day one | Access requested late, or the role was not specified | Raise access requests with the role and start date as soon as the contract is signed |
| Induction booked for the wrong week | The start date changed and nobody told L&D | Share any start-date change with HR, IT and L&D on the same day |
| Hiring manager unprepared | No first-week plan | Write the plan before day one |
| Starter goes quiet for weeks | No contact between offer and day one | Send a short update at every milestone |
| Starter left alone on day one | No buddy or schedule | Assign a buddy and agree a day-one timetable |

## Four principles

1. **No surprises.** The starter always knows what happens next and who to ask.
2. **One owner per task,** with a due date.
3. **Share status, not sensitive detail.** Say that a check is complete, not what it found.
4. **Ask for feedback while the experience is fresh.**
