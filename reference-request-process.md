# Reference Request Process (UK)

A step-by-step process for requesting, chasing, checking and recording employment references, with ready-to-use templates. Written for HR teams and hiring managers in UK organisations.

> [!NOTE]
> **Last reviewed:** October 2026. This is a general guide, not legal advice. Some sectors (for example regulated, health, social care and education roles) have specific reference requirements, so follow your sector's standards as well.

## The basics

- There is generally no legal duty to **give** a reference, but if you do give one it must be accurate, fair and not misleading.
- References are usually requested after a **conditional offer**, and the offer is made subject to satisfactory references.
- Ask the candidate's permission before contacting their **current** employer.
- Verify that each referee is genuine. Fake referees are a known risk.
- Treat references as confidential personal data: store them securely and limit who can see them.

## Suggested timeline

These are suggestions. Adjust them to your organisation.

| When | Action |
|---|---|
| Offer accepted | Candidate sends referee details and consent (template A) |
| Within 1-2 working days | Referee requests sent (template B) |
| After 3 working days | First chase (template C) |
| After 5 working days | Second chase, and ask the candidate to nudge their referee |
| After 7 working days | Escalate: ask the candidate for an alternative referee |
| Aim to finish within 10 working days | Review, record and mark the milestone complete |

---

## Step 1: Collect referee details and consent

- [ ] Candidate gives the details of the referees you need (check what your role or sector requires)
- [ ] Referee's name, job title, organisation, relationship to the candidate, and **work** email and phone number
- [ ] Candidate confirms in writing that you may contact each referee, and says whether the current employer can be contacted now or only after the offer is unconditional
- [ ] Employment dates and job titles match the candidate's CV

## Step 2: Verify the referee

- [ ] Referee uses an official organisational email address, not a personal one
- [ ] Contact details checked independently, for example through the organisation's switchboard or website
- [ ] Referee is a manager or HR contact who can speak to the candidate's work, not a friend or relative

## Step 3: Send the request

- [ ] Request sent with a clear deadline (template B)
- [ ] Date sent recorded
- [ ] Candidate told that requests have gone out

## Step 4: Chase

- [ ] First and second chases sent on time (template C)
- [ ] Candidate asked to nudge the referee if needed
- [ ] Escalated for an alternative referee if no reply after a week or so

## Step 5: Review the reference

- [ ] Dates, job title and duties match the CV and application
- [ ] Reason for leaving noted
- [ ] Performance, conduct and reliability comments reviewed
- [ ] Any concerns flagged to HR before any decision is made
- [ ] Safeguarding questions answered where the role requires them

## Step 6: Record and share the outcome

- [ ] Reference stored securely, with the date received and who reviewed it
- [ ] Outcome recorded: satisfactory / satisfactory with comments / not satisfactory
- [ ] Hiring manager told only that **references are complete**, with the date. Detail stays within HR

---

## If a reference is not satisfactory

> [!WARNING]
> Do not withdraw an offer on the strength of one comment without review.

- Take advice from HR before acting.
- Check the facts first. Ask the referee for specifics, and give the candidate a fair opportunity to respond where appropriate.
- Make sure the decision is consistent with how you treat other candidates, and does not relate to a protected characteristic.
- Record the reasoning.

## Red flags to follow up

- A personal email address, or a referee who will not confirm their organisation by other means
- A reference that is vague, or reads as identical to another
- Dates or job titles that do not match the CV
- A referee who is a friend, relative or the candidate themselves
- A referee who cannot be reached through the organisation's official contact details

## Templates

### A. Email to the candidate: referee details and consent

> **Subject:** Your references: next step
>
> Hi [name],
>
> Congratulations again on your offer. To move forward with your references, please reply with:
> - the name, job title, organisation and work email address of each referee, and how they know you;
> - confirmation that we may contact them;
> - whether we can contact your current employer now, or only once your offer is unconditional.
>
> Please let your referees know to expect our email. If you have any questions, reply to this message.
>
> Kind regards,
> [name]

### B. Email to the referee

> **Subject:** Reference request for [candidate name]
>
> Dear [referee name],
>
> [Candidate name] has been offered the position of [job title] at [organisation] and has given your name as a referee. Could you please complete the short form attached, or reply to the questions below, by [date]?
>
> 1. Confirm the candidate's job title and dates of employment.
> 2. Briefly describe their main duties.
> 3. Reason for leaving, if applicable.
> 4. How would you describe their performance, reliability and conduct?
> 5. Are you aware of any current disciplinary or conduct issues?
> 6. Would you re-employ them? If not, please say why.
> 7. Is there anything else relevant to this role?
>
> Your reply will be treated as confidential and used only for recruitment purposes. If you would prefer to talk it through by phone, please let me know a convenient time.
>
> Thank you for your help.
>
> [name, job title, organisation, contact details]

Only ask questions that are relevant to the role. Be careful with questions about health or sickness absence, as the law restricts health-related questions before a job offer and you should take advice on what is appropriate.

### C. Chase email to the referee

> **Subject:** Reminder: reference request for [candidate name]
>
> Dear [referee name],
>
> I'm following up on my email of [date] asking for a reference for [candidate name]. We would be grateful to receive it by [date], as it is the last item holding up their start. If it would be easier, I'm happy to take your answers by phone.
>
> Many thanks,
> [name]

## If you are asked to give a reference

- Check your organisation's policy first.
- Stick to facts you can support. Be accurate and fair, and do not mislead.
- Consider whether to give a factual reference only, confirming job title and dates.
- Keep a record of what you provided.
