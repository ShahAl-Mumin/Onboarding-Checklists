# Hiring manager guide: what to do between offer and day one

A short guide for managers who are not HR specialists. The weeks between "offer accepted" and a new starter's first day are easy to overlook, but what happens in them shapes how the new starter feels about the job.

> This is a generic guide, not legal advice. Check it against your own organisation's policies and ask HR if you're unsure.

---

## The short version

1. **Stay in touch with your new starter.** A short, friendly message from you means a lot.
2. **Know who owns what.** HR usually owns checks and contracts, IT owns equipment and access, and you own the welcome and the first-week plan.
3. **Do your part early.** The most common delays come from things that were not ordered or agreed in time.
4. **Ask HR for status, not detail.** You need to know if things are on track, not the detail of individual checks.

---

## Who does what

| Task | Usually owned by |
|---|---|
| Pre-employment checks (identity, right to work, references and others) | HR / recruitment team |
| Contract issued and signed | HR |
| Equipment, accounts and access | IT |
| Pay and benefits set-up | HR / payroll |
| First-week plan and welcome | **You** |
| Buddy or mentor | **You** |
| Team introductions | **You** |

If you aren't sure who owns something, ask HR early.

---

## Timeline

### As soon as the offer is accepted

- [ ] Ask HR for the planned start date and the main contact.
- [ ] Send the new starter a short welcome message. Say you're pleased they're joining and who to contact with questions.
- [ ] Tell your team who is joining, and when.
- [ ] Check what you need to request, such as equipment, access or a desk, and request it now.

### During the pre-start period

- [ ] Agree the first-week plan: who they will meet, what they will do on day one, what you expect in the first month.
- [ ] Choose a buddy or mentor and ask them if they're willing.
- [ ] Ask HR for a status update each week, and share any change in your own plans, such as holiday or absence in the first week.
- [ ] Answer any requests from HR or IT quickly. A day's delay from you can mean a late start for the new starter.

### One week before the start date

- [ ] Confirm the start time, location and who will meet them.
- [ ] Check that equipment, accounts and access are on track.
- [ ] Check that the first-week plan has been shared with the new starter and the buddy.
- [ ] Send a short message saying you're looking forward to seeing them.

### The day before

- [ ] Confirm that someone will be there to greet them.
- [ ] Send a short "see you tomorrow" message.

### Day one and the first week

- [ ] Be there to welcome them yourself, or make sure someone is.
- [ ] Introduce them to the team and show them where things are.
- [ ] Go through the first-week plan together.
- [ ] Check in at the end of day one, and again at the end of week one.

---

## What you should and should not expect from HR

**Reasonable things to ask for**

- The overall status: on track, at risk or delayed.
- What you need to do and when.
- A heads-up if the start date might change.

**Things you usually should not ask for**

- The detail of an individual check, such as what a reference said or the outcome of a criminal record or health check. HR normally handles these confidentially and tells you only what you need to know.

---

## Common problems and how to avoid them

| Problem | How to avoid it |
|---|---|
| Equipment or access isn't ready on day one | Request it as soon as the offer is accepted, and check a week before |
| New starter hears nothing for weeks | Send a short message yourself, and ask HR what they have shared |
| Start date slips with little warning | Ask HR for weekly updates, and flag any risk early |
| Nobody is there on day one | Confirm who will meet them, and confirm it again the day before |
| The first week has no plan | Agree a simple plan before they start |
| Manager is away in the first week | Tell HR early and name someone to cover |

---

## Questions to ask HR

- Is the start date still on track?
- What is still outstanding, and who owns it?
- Is there anything you need from me?
- When will I next get an update?
- What happens if a check takes longer than expected?

---

## One-page summary

**Before day one:** welcome message, request equipment and access, agree the first-week plan, choose a buddy.
**Week before:** confirm the details and check everything is ready.
**Day one:** be there, introduce the team, go through the plan.
**First week:** check in at the end of day one and the end of the week.
