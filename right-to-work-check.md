# Right to Work Check (UK): Process Checklist

A step-by-step guide to running a right to work check, from choosing the route to recording the outcome. Written for HR teams and hiring managers in UK organisations.

> [!NOTE]
> **Last reviewed:** October 2026. This is a general guide, not legal advice. Home Office rules change, and changes to the scheme were scheduled from 1 October 2026, so always follow the current official guidance on GOV.UK and confirm what applies to your hires.

## Why it matters

- Employers must prevent illegal working. Carrying out the correct check, in the correct way, gives you a "statutory excuse" against a civil penalty.
- The maximum civil penalty is up to £45,000 per illegal worker for a first breach and up to £60,000 for repeat breaches.
- The employer is liable, not the member of staff who carried out the check.
- Checks must be completed **before** employment starts.

## Seven golden rules

1. **Check everyone in the same way,** whatever their name, accent or appearance. Selective checking can be discrimination.
2. **Check before the first day of work.**
3. **Use the correct route** for the person (see step 1).
4. **Check the person is the rightful holder:** the photo matches them and the date of birth is consistent.
5. **Record the date of the check** and keep a secure copy.
6. **Diarise any follow-up check** if the person's permission is time-limited.
7. **Never rely on a verbal assurance** or an unverified copy.

## Step 1: Choose the right route

| Route | Typically used for | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| Home Office online check | People with digital immigration status (for example an eVisa) and some others | The person generates a share code from their account. You enter the code and their date of birth on GOV.UK to view their right to work details |
| Digital identity check (certified provider) | British and Irish citizens with a valid passport | A certified identity service provider checks the passport digitally and gives you an outcome to keep |
| Manual document check | People who can show acceptable original documents (List A or List B on GOV.UK), where a manual check is allowed | You check the original documents in the holder's presence |
| Employer Checking Service | People who cannot show documents or a share code, for example while an application is pending | You ask the Home Office to confirm their right to work |

Which route applies depends on the person's status and documents. Confirm against the current employer's guide on GOV.UK.

## Step 2: Obtain, check, copy

### Obtain

- Ask for the share code and date of birth, or the original documents, depending on the route.
- Share codes expire after a set period, so ask for a fresh one if there is a delay.

### Check

- **Online or digital check:** confirm the photo matches the person, in person or by live video call.
- **Manual check:** look at the originals in the holder's presence and confirm that:
  - the photo and date of birth are consistent with the person in front of you;
  - the documents have not expired or been altered;
  - names match across documents, or there is evidence for any difference (such as a marriage certificate);
  - the permission covers the work you are offering, including any restrictions on hours.

### Copy and record

- **Manual check:** take a clear copy of each document in a format that cannot be altered, and record the date of the check and who carried it out.
- **Online or digital check:** keep the output showing the date of the check.
- Keep the records for the whole of the person's employment and for two years afterwards.
- Store them securely, with access limited to those who need it.

## Step 3: Follow-up checks

- **British and Irish citizens:** no follow-up check is needed.
- **Time-limited permission:** put the expiry date in the diary and check again before the permission ends.
- If you cannot confirm continuing right to work, take advice before the person carries on working.

## Common mistakes

- Checking after the person has already started
- Only checking people you assume are from overseas
- Accepting photocopies or scans for a manual check, when originals are required
- Not recording the date of the check
- Not diarising expiry dates for time-limited permission
- Using the wrong route for the person
- Keeping copies in a shared inbox or an unsecured folder

## Sharing the outcome

Hiring managers need to know that the check is complete, not the person's documents or immigration status. Share "Right to work: complete" with the date, and keep the detail within HR. Apply data protection principles: collect only what you need, store it securely, and keep it only as long as the rules require.
