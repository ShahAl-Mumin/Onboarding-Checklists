# Offer-to-start message templates

Plain-English messages for the weeks between "offer accepted" and a new starter's first day. This is often a quiet period for the new starter, and a few clear messages can make a big difference.

Replace anything in [square brackets], and edit the wording so it sounds like your organisation.

> These templates are a starting point. They are generic, they are not legal advice, and you should check them against your own policies before using them.

---

## How to use these

- **Say what is happening, who owns it and when you will update next.** People worry less when they know what to expect.
- **Name one contact.** Give the new starter one person to reply to, not a general inbox.
- **Keep it short.** One or two things per message.
- **Keep personal details out of email.** Do not describe the detail of a check in an email, and do not share it with the hiring manager (see the notes at the end).

---

## Part A: Messages to the new starter

### 1. Offer accepted: thank you and what happens next

**Subject:** Welcome to [organisation]: what happens next

Hi [first name],

Thank you for accepting the offer for the [job title] role. We're really pleased you're joining us.

Here's what happens between now and your first day:

1. **We'll ask you for some documents and forms.** You'll get a separate message from me with exactly what we need.
2. **We'll complete the pre-employment checks.** I'll keep you updated as they progress.
3. **We'll send your contract.** [Add expected timing if you can.]
4. **We'll confirm your start details** a week or so before you start.

Your start date is currently [date]. I'm your main contact until you start, so please reply to this email or call me on [number] if you have any questions.

Kind regards,
[Name], [team]

---

### 2. What we need from you

**Subject:** What we need from you before you start

Hi [first name],

To keep things moving, please send us the following by [date]:

- [Document or form 1]
- [Document or form 2]
- [Document or form 3]

[Explain how to send them, for example through a secure upload link. Please do not ask people to send documents by ordinary email unless your policy allows it.]

If you can't provide something, or you're not sure what counts, let me know and I'll help.

Kind regards,
[Name]

---

### 3. Checks update: in progress

**Subject:** An update on your pre-employment checks

Hi [first name],

A quick update: your pre-employment checks are in progress.

- **Complete:** [for example, identity and right to work]
- **Still in progress:** [for example, references]
- **Waiting on you:** [anything they need to do, or "nothing needed from you at the moment"]

I'll update you again by [date].

Kind regards,
[Name]

---

### 4. We're waiting on something (delay)

**Subject:** Your start date: a short update

Hi [first name],

I wanted to let you know that we're still waiting for [a general reason, for example "one of the checks to come back"]. This may affect [the timing of your contract / your start date].

Here's what happens next: [what you are doing, and who is chasing it]. I'll update you again by [date], even if there's no news.

I'm sorry for the wait, and I'm happy to talk it through if it would help.

Kind regards,
[Name]

---

### 5. Your contract is on its way

**Subject:** Your contract

Hi [first name],

Your contract for the [job title] role is attached / available at [link]. Please read it, and let me know if you have any questions.

To accept it, please [sign / return it] by [date]. Once we have it, you'll be ready for the next stage.

Kind regards,
[Name]

---

### 6. One week to go

**Subject:** Your first week at [organisation]

Hi [first name],

You start on [date], so here's what to expect.

- **Where to go and when:** [location, time, who to ask for]
- **Who you'll meet:** [line manager, buddy, team]
- **What to bring:** [anything required]
- **Your first day:** [short outline]
- **Equipment and access:** [what will be ready, and what will be set up on day one]

If anything changes, I'll let you know straight away. See you soon.

Kind regards,
[Name]

---

### 7. The day before you start

**Subject:** See you tomorrow

Hi [first name],

Just a quick message to say we're looking forward to seeing you tomorrow. [One line on where to go and who will meet them.] If anything comes up tonight or in the morning, call [name] on [number].

Kind regards,
[Name]

## Part B: Messages to the hiring manager

### 8. Offer accepted: your new starter's plan

**Subject:** [Name] has accepted: what happens next

Hi [manager name],

[Name] has accepted the offer for [job title], with a planned start date of [date].

**What happens next**

| Step | Owner | Target date |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-employment checks | [HR] | [date] |
| Contract issued and signed | [HR] | [date] |
| Equipment and access ordered | [IT] | [date] |
| First-week plan agreed | You | [date] |

**What we need from you:** [for example, confirm the first-week plan, nominate a buddy, confirm who will meet [name]].

I'll send you a short update on [day each week] until [name] starts.

Kind regards,
[Name]

---

### 9. Status update: "where is my new starter up to?"

**Subject:** Update on [Name]'s start

Hi [manager name],

A quick update on [Name], who starts on [date].

- **Overall:** [On track / At risk]
- **Checks:** [for example, "on track" or "complete"]
- **Contract:** [for example, "issued" or "signed"]
- **Equipment and access:** [status]
- **What I need from you:** [action, or "nothing at the moment"]

I'll send the next update on [date].

Kind regards,
[Name]

*Note: share the overall status, not the detail of any individual check.*

---

### 10. Action needed from you

**Subject:** Action needed: [Name]'s start on [date]

Hi [manager name],

To keep [Name]'s start on track, I need you to [action] by [date].

If it isn't done by then, [consequence, for example "their equipment may not be ready on day one"]. Let me know if you'd like to talk it through.

Kind regards,
[Name]

---

## Notes

- **Be consistent.** Use the same wording and update rhythm for every starter so nobody gets a different experience.
- **Hiring managers do not usually need the detail of checks.** Share the overall status (for example "on track" or "complete") and what is needed from them. Details such as health information or criminal record outcomes are normally handled by HR and shared only where your policy says it is necessary.
- **Keep records of what you send,** for example in your tracker or case notes.
- **Review them regularly.** Update the wording when your process changes.
