# Right to work and visa sponsorship: a plain-English overview

A short, practical overview for HR, recruiters and hiring managers in the UK. It explains the basic steps and where to find the official rules.

> **Not legal advice.** UK immigration and right to work rules change often. The Home Office updated its *Employer's guide to right to work checks* with effect from 1 October 2026, and further updates are possible. Always check the current official guidance on GOV.UK, and get specialist advice for complicated cases.
>
> *Last reviewed: October 2026.*

---

## Part 1: Right to work checks

### What they are

A right to work check confirms that a person is allowed to do the work they have been offered in the UK. Employers must do it **before the person starts work**, for every new employee, and it must be done the same way for everyone.

### The three main ways to check

| Method | Who it is generally for | In outline |
|---|---|---|
| **Online check with a share code** | People with digital immigration status (such as an eVisa), and others who can generate a share code | The person gives you their share code and date of birth, and you check on the GOV.UK service |
| **Manual check of original documents** | People with acceptable physical documents, for example a passport | You see the original documents, check them in the person's presence (in person or by video, depending on the route), and take and keep a copy |
| **Certified digital identity service provider (IDSP)** | British and Irish citizens with a valid passport or passport card | A certified provider checks the document digitally on your behalf |

Which method applies depends on the person's documents and status, so check the current guidance for each case.

### What to record and keep

- A copy of the evidence, or the online check result
- The date you made the check
- Who made the check

Keep records for the length of the person's employment and for **two years after** they leave, unless the current guidance says otherwise.

### Follow-up checks

If someone's permission to work is time-limited, you may need a follow-up check before it expires. Diarise the date when you do the first check.

### Do it fairly

- Check **everyone** in the same way. Don't decide who to check based on someone's name, appearance, accent or nationality.
- Don't ask for extra documents that aren't needed.
- If a person can't show documents straight away, tell them what they can do and what the deadline is, and ask for specialist advice where needed.

### Penalties

Getting this wrong can lead to civil penalties and other consequences for an employer. The amounts and rules change, so check the current figures on GOV.UK.

### Recent changes to be aware of

From 1 October 2026 the Home Office has expanded the scope of right to work responsibilities for some working arrangements that are not traditional employment. If you engage workers through other arrangements, read the current guide to see whether the rules affect you.

## Part 2: Sponsoring a worker from overseas

### The basic idea

Many people coming to the UK to work need to be **sponsored** by an employer that holds a sponsor licence. This overview describes the general route for sponsored workers. The detailed requirements, including the roles, salaries and skill levels that qualify, change often, so check them for each role.

### Outline of the process

1. **Check the employer can sponsor.** The organisation needs a valid sponsor licence for the relevant route.
2. **Check the role is eligible.** Roles must meet the current requirements for the route. Check this *before* promising a job or a start date.
3. **Make the job offer,** making clear that it depends on the person getting permission to work.
4. **Assign a Certificate of Sponsorship (CoS).** This is a digital record that the sponsor creates for the worker through the Home Office system. The worker uses its reference number in their visa application.
5. **The worker applies for their visa,** usually online. They will normally pay application fees and may pay other charges. Check which charges apply and who pays them.
6. **Wait for the decision.** Timescales vary. Don't confirm a start date until permission is granted.
7. **Check right to work before they start.** Once the worker has permission, complete a right to work check as in Part 1 (often through a share code for digital status).
8. **Meet your sponsor duties.** Sponsors have continuing duties, such as keeping records and reporting certain changes. Check the current sponsor guidance.

### Sample timeline for a sponsored starter

| When | What happens | Usually owned by |
|---|---|---|
| Before the offer | Check the role is eligible and the organisation can sponsor | HR / recruitment, with specialist advice if needed |
| Offer accepted | Explain the process and timing to the candidate | HR / recruitment |
| After acceptance | Gather the information for the CoS and assign it | HR / sponsor licence manager |
| Visa application | Candidate applies and you support them with questions | Candidate, with HR support |
| Decision received | Confirm the start date, and complete the right to work check | HR |
| Before day one | Plan travel, accommodation questions, equipment and welcome | HR, hiring manager, IT |
| After they start | Meet continuing sponsor duties, keep records | HR / sponsor licence manager |

### Tips for a smoother process

- **Start early.** Sponsored hires take longer than standard ones, and the dates are less certain.
- **Be honest about timing.** Say clearly that the start date depends on the visa decision.
- **Keep the candidate updated.** Uncertainty is stressful. Regular updates are especially important here.
- **Name one contact** for the candidate and the hiring manager.
- **Keep clear records** of what was done and when.
- **Take advice on complicated cases,** such as previous refusals or a candidate already in the UK on another visa.
