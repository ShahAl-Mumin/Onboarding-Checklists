# Pre-Employment Checks: Overview (UK)

A plain-English overview of the checks UK employers commonly run before a new starter begins, how to decide which ones a role needs, and how to run them in a sensible order. Written for HR teams and hiring managers.

> [!NOTE]
> **Last reviewed:** October 2026. This is a general guide, not legal advice. Requirements vary by sector and role, and rules change, so follow the current official guidance and your sector's standards (for example NHS employment check standards, safeguarding rules in education and social care, or government security vetting policy).

## Principles

1. **Proportionate.** Only run checks the role genuinely needs. Some checks (such as criminal record checks) are only lawful for eligible roles.
2. **Consistent.** Apply the same checks to everyone in the same role.
3. **After a conditional offer.** Most checks happen once an offer has been made subject to satisfactory outcomes. Be careful with health questions before an offer: the Equality Act 2010 restricts them.
4. **Transparent.** Tell candidates which checks you will run, why, and what happens to their data.
5. **Share status, not detail.** Hiring managers need to know a check is complete or in progress, not what it found.
6. **Secure.** Collect only what you need, store it securely, limit access, and follow your retention policy.

## The checks at a glance

| Check | What it confirms | Usually needed for | Typical owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identity | The person is who they say they are | Every role | HR |
| [Right to work](right-to-work-check.md) | The person is allowed to do the work | Every role | HR |
| [References](reference-request-process.md) | Employment history, conduct and reliability | Most roles | HR |
| Employment history and gaps | The CV is complete and consistent | Most roles | HR |
| Qualifications and registration | Required qualifications and professional registration are genuine and current | Roles that require them | HR / Hiring manager |
| Criminal record (DBS and equivalents) | Relevant convictions, cautions and barring information | Eligible roles only | HR / DBS provider |
| Occupational health | Fitness for the role, and any adjustments needed | Many roles, and always where adjustments are requested | Occupational health |
| Security vetting | Suitability for access to government information or sites | Government and some contractor roles | Vetting team |
| Overseas checks | Criminal record and history from other countries | Applicants who have lived or worked abroad | HR |

## Criminal record checks

### Levels (England and Wales)

| Level | What it covers | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Basic | Unspent convictions and conditional cautions | Roles that are not eligible for a higher level |
| Standard | Spent and unspent convictions, plus cautions, reprimands and warnings (some filtered) | Certain regulated roles and professions |
| Enhanced | Standard, plus relevant information held by police | Roles working with children or adults at risk |
| Enhanced with barred list check | Enhanced, plus a check of the children's and/or adults' barred lists | Regulated activity with children or adults at risk |

Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own bodies (Disclosure Scotland and AccessNI).

### Things to know

- **Eligibility decides the level.** You must not ask for a check the role is not eligible for. Check the current DBS guidance for your role.
- **Use a registered or umbrella body** to submit applications if your organisation is not registered itself.
- **The certificate goes to the applicant.** The employer sees it by asking the applicant to show it, or by using the DBS Update Service where the applicant has subscribed and consents.
- **Handle certificate information carefully.** Do not keep it longer than necessary. DBS guidance is generally to keep it for no more than six months, although you can keep a record of the date, certificate number and your decision. Check the current code of practice.
- **Do not treat a conviction as an automatic bar.** Assess relevance, the nature of the role and the time elapsed, using a written policy.

### Checklist

- [ ] Role checked against DBS eligibility, and the correct level chosen
- [ ] Candidate told which check will be run and why
- [ ] Application started through the registered or umbrella body
- [ ] Candidate's ID verified as the DBS process requires
- [ ] Certificate seen (or Update Service status checked), with the candidate's consent
- [ ] Date, certificate number and decision recorded; certificate not retained longer than needed
- [ ] Hiring manager told "criminal record check: complete" only

## Occupational health

- Usually carried out **after a conditional offer** with a health questionnaire, and sometimes a consultation.
- Its purpose is to confirm fitness for the role and to identify **reasonable adjustments**, not to screen people out.
- The employer and hiring manager should receive only an outcome, for example **cleared**, **cleared with adjustments** (with the adjustments listed) or **not yet cleared** with a recommendation. They should not receive medical detail.
- If a candidate asks for adjustments, arrange them before day one wherever possible.

### Checklist

- [ ] Role checked for any health requirements
- [ ] Questionnaire sent after the conditional offer, with a clear explanation of how the information will be used
- [ ] Outcome received from occupational health (outcome only, no medical detail)
- [ ] Adjustments agreed with the candidate and put in place before the start date
- [ ] Hiring manager told "occupational health: complete" and any adjustments they need to put in place

---

## Qualifications and professional registration

- [ ] Original certificates seen for any qualifications the role requires
- [ ] Qualifications verified with the awarding body or institution where the risk or role justifies it
- [ ] Professional registration checked on the public register on the date of the check (for example nursing, medical, allied health or legal registers)
- [ ] Any conditions or restrictions on registration noted
- [ ] Date of check and who carried it out recorded

## Employment history

- [ ] CV compared with the application form and the references
- [ ] Gaps in employment explained and noted
- [ ] Dates and job titles consistent across documents

## Government security vetting

Roles in government and some contractor roles require security clearance. The baseline standard covers identity, right to work, employment history and unspent criminal convictions. Higher levels (such as SC and DV) involve more detailed checks and take longer. Follow your vetting team's guidance, and start early.

## Applicants from overseas

- [ ] Overseas criminal record certificates requested where the applicant has lived or worked abroad (requirements vary by country)
- [ ] Documents translated where needed
- [ ] If the role is sponsored, the sponsorship steps and their lead times built into the plan

## Running the checks in the right order

1. **Day of conditional offer:** send the candidate a clear list of the checks, why each is needed and what you need from them.
2. **Start the long-lead checks first.** Criminal record checks, security vetting, overseas checks and occupational health can all take time, and timescales vary.
3. **Run the quick checks in parallel:** identity, right to work, references and qualifications.
4. **Review as outcomes arrive.** Record each outcome with a date.
5. **Confirm that everything required is complete** before the start date, and tell the hiring manager at milestone level.
6. **Keep the candidate informed** at each stage so they are never left guessing.

## What hiring managers need to see

Use simple statuses: **not required**, **in progress**, **complete**. Keep documents, outcomes and any concerns within HR.
